# Performance

How this site stays fast, what was changed to make it so, and what you have to
do to keep it that way.

The audience matters here and drove most of the decisions below: a large share
of visitors reach this site from Chitral and the surrounding valleys on mobile
connections. Bytes and round trips are not an abstract score — they are whether
the page arrives.

---

## Results

Measured against commit `7ceafda` checked out into a parallel worktree and
served by the same Apache, so the two numbers are directly comparable. Text
resources are counted gzipped (the real host runs mod_deflate/mod_brotli; this
XAMPP loads neither, so they were gzipped locally to get the number a visitor
actually receives). Images are counted as the WebP a modern browser is served.
"Initial load" is what the browser fetches before the visitor scrolls.

| Homepage | Before | After |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Initial load | ~3,879 KB | **203 KB** |
| Requests | 19 | **10** |
| Origins contacted | 3 | **1** |

The before figure is 3,109 KB of directly measurable resources plus two things
a naive scan misses and a browser does not:

- **679 KB** of hero slides carrying `loading="lazy"`. Every slide is
  `position: absolute; inset: 0`, so all nine were *inside* the viewport, and
  lazy loading only defers images outside it. The attribute did nothing.
- **91 KB** of Google Fonts (17.8 KB of CSS plus 73.8 KB of woff2), requested
  by an `@import` inside `styles.css` where no HTML scan can see it.

Interior pages are roughly unchanged in bytes (contact: ~148 KB before,
147 KB after) because the fonts they were already downloading are now counted
honestly against us. What changed there is latency and caching, not size: one
origin instead of three, fonts discovered in the initial HTML instead of after
a stylesheet parse, and CSS that no longer blocks the first paint.

Nothing about the site's appearance or behaviour changed.

## What was done

### Images

The single largest category, and the least interesting: most of it was waste
rather than anything clever.

- **4.9 MB of unreferenced images deleted.** Six 1024×1024 PNGs
  (`project-*.png`) that no page, stylesheet or script referenced.
- **WebP twins for every raster source**, generated by
  `tools/optimize-images.php` and served by content negotiation (below).
  Full-size twins are 53% smaller in aggregate; PNGs do far better than that
  (a 1.9 MB team photo became 72 KB).
- **Responsive width variants** at 480/768/1200/1600 px, emitted as `srcset`
  by `imageTag()`. A phone gets a 40 KB hero instead of a 613 KB one.
- **Five fake SVGs converted to real images** — see the section below.
- **`decoding="async"`** on all 74 `<img>` tags, so image decode stays off the
  main thread. The LCP image is `decoding="sync"` deliberately: an async
  decode there can land a frame after paint.

### The hero slideshow

The homepage hero was downloading all nine of its slides — about 2.4 MB —
before the visitor could see the first one. `loading="lazy"` was present on
five of them and, as described above, could not work.

Only slide 1 now carries a real `src`. The rest hold their URL in `data-src`,
and `initSlideshow()` in `main.js` hydrates each one slide ahead of the
rotation. The first look-ahead waits for the `load` event so it never competes
with the LCP image; rotation is 8 seconds, so there is ample time.

Slide 1 is `loading="eager"`, `fetchpriority="high"`, and preloaded from
`<head>` with matching `imagesrcset`/`imagesizes`. The matching part is
load-bearing: preloading a bare `src` while the tag carries a `srcset` makes
the browser fetch *both*, which is worse than no preload at all.

### Fake SVGs

Five files named `.svg`, served as `image/svg+xml`, contained no vector data —
just a base64-encoded PNG in an `<svg>` wrapper, which also inflates the PNG by
a third:

| File | Was | Displayed at | Now (WebP) |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: |
| `logo.svg` | 102 KB | 52×52 | **15 KB** |
| `STUDENTS.svg` | 284 KB | 100×100 | **5 KB** |
| `Years of Experience.svg` | 274 KB | 100×100 | **5 KB** |
| `LEARNING SPACES.svg` | 213 KB | 100×100 | **6 KB** |
| `TEACHERS.svg` | 174 KB | 100×100 | **4 KB** |

The logo was in the header of all 24 pages, above the fold. `tools/derasterize-svg.php`
converts them; the originals are left on disk and are no longer referenced.

### Fonts

Fonts were loaded by `@import url(https://fonts.googleapis.com/...)` on line 7
of `styles.css`. That is the slowest available option: an `@import` is
invisible to the browser's preload scanner, so the fonts were not discovered
until `styles.css` had been downloaded *and parsed*, and then took two more
serial round trips to two origins that needed their own DNS lookup, TCP
connection and TLS handshake. Four serial round trips before a glyph could
paint.

They are now self-hosted (`tools/fetch-fonts.php`), which:

- removes two third-party origins from every page;
- lets the two faces used above the fold be `<link rel="preload">`ed from the
  initial HTML;
- puts them under the year-long immutable cache rule already in `.htaccess`;
- allowed the CSP to drop to `font-src 'self'` with no Google host at all.

Unused weights were dropped (Inter 300 — no rule in `styles.css` sets a weight
below 400) and non-Latin subsets were not downloaded. The admin panel had the
same `@import` problem with DM Sans and got the same treatment.

`font-display: swap` is preserved from upstream, so text paints immediately in
the fallback face rather than being invisible while the web font downloads.

### CSS

`styles.css` is 159 KB and was render-blocking: nothing painted until it
arrived and parsed.

`tools/build-critical-css.php` extracts the above-the-fold subset — 26 KB raw,
**5.9 KB gzipped** — which is inlined into `<head>`, and the full stylesheet is
then loaded without blocking via `media="print"` + `onload`. The two changes
are a pair; either alone is useless or actively worse.

The critical subset is *generated* from the real stylesheet rather than
hand-copied, because a hand-maintained copy is wrong the first time somebody
edits `styles.css` and forgets, and the failure mode is a flash of unstyled
header on every page. Only the list of selector prefixes is maintained by hand.

`.reveal` / `.reveal-left` / `.reveal-right` are in the critical set even
though the elements they apply to are mostly below the fold. They start at
`opacity: 0`, so leaving them out inverts what a visitor sees: content paints
visible, then the stylesheet arrives and hides it, then JavaScript fades it
back in.

### JavaScript

- All scripts `defer`red.
- **Leaflet is no longer in the page.** It was a stylesheet in `<head>` from
  unpkg.com and a 43 KB script before `</body>`, on the homepage and
  `/early-childhood`, loaded whether or not the visitor ever scrolled to the
  map. `map.js` now injects both — with the same SRI hashes — when the map is
  within 400 px of the viewport. ~57 KB and a third-party connection removed
  from the initial load of two pages.
- `<img id="lightbox-img" src="">` fixed. An empty `src` is not an empty
  image: browsers resolve it against the document URL and request the **page
  again**, a second full HTML download on every visit to the homepage and
  `/gallery`.

### Server (`public/.htaccess`)

- **WebP content negotiation.** A request for `photo.jpg` is served
  `photo.jpg.webp` when the browser's `Accept` header allows it and the twin
  exists on disk. One rule covers all 74 `<img>` tags, every `srcset`
  candidate, every CMS upload, and every `background-image` in `styles.css` —
  which markup cannot reach at all. `Vary: Accept` is set on the image
  extensions so a shared proxy cannot hand WebP to a client that cannot read
  it.
- **Brotli** added ahead of the existing gzip (~15–20% better on this site's
  text; `styles.css` goes to about 25 KB against gzip's 33 KB).
- Already-compressed formats excluded from both compressors.
- `AddType` for `image/webp` and `font/woff2`, for hosts whose `mime.types`
  lacks them.

### Cache busting

`.htaccess` serves CSS and JS with `Cache-Control: immutable, max-age=1 year`,
which means a browser will not revalidate them for a year — not on reload, not
on a hard refresh. That is only safe if a change ships under a new URL, and the
site was half doing it: `styles.css` had a hand-typed `?v=4.3` while
`uiverse-cards.css` and `impact-goals-new.css` had nothing. **Editing either of
those two would have been invisible to every returning visitor for a year, with
no way to push a fix.**

`asset()` now derives the version from the file's own mtime. Nothing to
remember, nothing to bump by hand.

### Database

Already well indexed — every public query is covered by a composite index on
`(status, deleted_at, display_order, …)`, and there is no N+1 pattern (sponsors
and their groups are two queries, not one per group). The only change needed
was a per-request static cache in `siteSettings()`, which three pages were
calling twice per load.

## Keeping it fast

### After adding or replacing an image in the repository

```
php -d extension=gd tools/optimize-images.php
```

Generates any missing WebP twins and width variants. Re-runs are cheap — a
derivative newer than its source is skipped, so changing one photo re-encodes
that photo and nothing else. Add `--force` to rebuild everything.

Images uploaded through the **admin panel need nothing**: the same code runs at
upload time (`public/admin/includes/upload.php`), and derivatives are deleted
along with the source.

### After editing `public/css/styles.css`

```
php tools/build-critical-css.php
```

Otherwise the inlined above-the-fold CSS drifts from the stylesheet it was
extracted from. `--list-dropped` shows what was excluded.

### After changing which fonts or weights are used

```
php tools/fetch-fonts.php
```

Edit `FONT_SHEETS` in that script first — it is the source of truth. Downloads
only what is missing.

### If someone adds another design-tool "SVG"

```
php -d extension=gd tools/derasterize-svg.php          # report
php -d extension=gd tools/derasterize-svg.php --apply  # convert
```

Reports any `.svg` that is really a wrapped bitmap. Worth running if the icons
directory ever grows unexpectedly.

### Never

- Do not reintroduce an `@import` for fonts in `styles.css` or `admin.css`.
  Both files carry a comment saying so and why.
- Do not add a hardcoded `?v=` to a stylesheet or script URL. Use `asset()`.
- Do not add a CDN origin to only one copy of the CSP. There are two — the
  `<meta>` in `includes/layout/head-meta.php` and the header in
  `public/.htaccess` — and the browser enforces their intersection, so adding
  a source to one changes nothing.

## Local environment notes

- **`gd` is disabled** in this XAMPP's `php.ini` (line 931, `;extension=gd`).
  The image tools therefore need `-d extension=gd` on the command line.
  Hostinger enables `gd` by default, so the CMS upload path works there without
  configuration. Nothing the *site* serves needs `gd` at request time.
- **`mod_deflate`, `mod_expires` and `mod_brotli` are not loaded** in this
  XAMPP's `httpd.conf`, so the compression and caching blocks in
  `public/.htaccess` do nothing locally. They are wrapped in `<IfModule>` and
  take effect on the real host. Sizes measured locally are uncompressed unless
  gzipped by hand.

## Worth doing next

Not done here, in rough order of value:

1. **A purpose-made 1200×630 social card.** `og:image` currently points at a
   1800×1350 programme photo — the best asset in the repository, but the wrong
   aspect ratio for the platforms that crop it.
2. **Split `styles.css`.** 159 KB is one file for 24 pages; the homepage-only
   and programme-page-only rules could be separated so each page ships less.
   Worth doing only with a real usage audit behind it.
3. **Drop `'unsafe-inline'` from the CSP.** Blocked by 99 inline `style=`
   attributes and 24 inline event handlers, not by the header. A code change,
   not a header change.
4. **Delete the five orphaned `.svg` files** once you are satisfied nothing
   external links to them.
5. **Field measurement.** Everything above is lab-measured. Real Core Web
   Vitals from real devices on real Chitral connections would be more useful
   than any of it.
