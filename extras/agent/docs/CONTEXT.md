## 2. What we are trying to achieve

**Primary goal: donations.** Confirmed by the project owner. Every UX decision resolves in favour of moving a visitor toward giving.

Two audiences, with a deliberate priority:

1. **US individual donors** (primary) — need emotional connection, then proof their money is well spent. Includes a **Muslim-American segment for whom Zakat eligibility is a decisive factor**; no US-based peer handles this well, so it is an open advantage.
2. **Institutions** — grant-makers, universities, government partners. Need research, reports, transparency, partnership legitimacy. Served by depth on subpages, not by diluting the homepage.

### The conversion facts that should drive design

From published research (NN/G, M+R Benchmarks 2025, NextAfter):

- Donors arrive with exactly two questions: **what do you stand for**, and **how will you spend my money**. Only 43% of tested nonprofit homepages answer the first; **only 4% answer the second.** Answering the second, prominently, is the largest differentiator available to us.
- Mission clarity was **3.6× more important** than any other factor when donors chose between charities.
- Donation pages convert at ~11% desktop / ~8% mobile. ~43% of gifts come from mobile, and mobile converts 30–40% worse — so mobile is where the money leaks.
- Average one-time gift ~$126; average monthly gift ~$24. **A monthly donor overtakes a one-time donor in 5.25 months.** Monthly giving deserves its own identity and destination, not a checkbox.
- Abandonment is roughly **47% design problems / 53% content problems**. Fix clarity before optimising the form.
- Tie each ask amount to a **different** impact statement. Generic taglines underperform; one such test lifted conversion 39.1%.
- **The identifiable-victim effect**: one named person out-raises statistics — and putting statistics *inside* the story block suppresses giving by triggering deliberative thinking. So: **story alone in the emotional moment, aggregate numbers in a separate later section.**

## 3. What the site should feel like

**"Competent partnership," never "rescue."**

The test for any element: does this make an American donor feel like a **co-investor in a capable local institution**, or like a **savior of helpless children**? The second framing is both ethically wrong and commercially weaker against peers.

Borrowed framing that fits WEF exactly (from Aga Khan Foundation USA): *locally led · here for generations · thinking holistically · relying on you.*

**Visual language.** Chitral itself is the differentiator — high-altitude Hindu Kush light, stone, snow, deep blues and warm ochres. That palette lets the site be *beautiful* without being *tragic*. Typography stays quiet and editorial; photography carries the emotion.

**Interaction feel.** Calm and substantial. Scroll-driven reveals, generous spacing, motion that settles rather than bounces. Institutional seriousness is what makes a small Dallas nonprofit credible against TCF-scale peers. No urgency pop-ups, no bouncing counters, no confetti.

### Ethical constraints — non-negotiable

WEF's beneficiaries are **minors, many of them girls, in a conservative rural region where girls' education is contested.** Identifiability carries real personal risk. We follow the **CONCORD / Dóchas Code of Conduct on Images and Messages** and the Markkula Center's ethical storytelling guidance:

- Photograph people as **agents, not victims** — mid-activity, eye-level, engaged. Strength, community, ordinary life.
- **Never** use downcast eyes, dirt-smeared faces, bare feet as shorthand, outstretched hands, or a Western visitor at the centre of the frame.
- Never publish a child's face without documented guardian consent. Record per subject whether they consent to being **named** and being **identifiable** — these are two separate permissions.
- Let subjects narrate their own stories. Byline them. "Nothing about us without us."
- WEF is the facilitator, not the hero. Chitral is the subject.

## 4. Positioning against peers

Researched: The Citizens Foundation, Developments in Literacy, Zindagi Trust, AKDN / Aga Khan Foundation USA, Room to Read, Pratham USA, Malala Fund, and charity: water as the global gold standard.

**Where we can win:**

- **Named specificity.** Every peer is vague about place. AKF USA doesn't even mention Chitral or Pakistan on its homepage. We can put **one named valley on a map with real pins** — centers, model schools, learning hubs — and make our purpose checkable.
- **The money answer.** Publish program-expense percentage, EIN, and Candid / Charity Navigator standing high on the page, not buried in the footer. **Confirmed 2026-09-22: WEF holds none of these third-party ratings yet** (Charity Navigator, Candid/GuideStar, CharityWatch) — do not display rating badges. The "100% direct program funding" + EIN claims already on the homepage donate section are real and usable; a rating badge is not, until WEF actually has one.
- ~~Zakat eligibility.~~ **Confirmed 2026-09-22: not currently established.** Researched as a genuine, unclaimed advantage versus US peers, but this is a religious/legal determination about WEF's own fund use that the project cannot verify or assert. No Zakat claim has been added. Worth pursuing a scholarly ruling separately if the client wants this advantage later — see the donation-conversion note below.
- **Institutional embedding.** The university and government partnerships prove we are inside a functioning system, not parachuting in.

**What to avoid, learned from peers' mistakes:** internal announcements occupying donor-facing hero space; duplicated desktop/mobile content blocks; statistics inside story blocks; leading with founding history or the board. (The "placeholder `0+` counters" item that was here is corrected in §8 defect #3 — they are not placeholders, and whether production's JS is actually broken is unconfirmed from this session.)

**Worth stealing:** charity: water's problem-first headline, live progress meter, exact-not-rounded numbers, and named monthly-giving tier ("The Spring"); Malala Fund's bylined beneficiary authorship; DIL's visible program-expense percentage; Room to Read's discipline of *one* homepage statistic.

### Donation-conversion build — 2026-09-22

Confirmed with the client before building anything that touches trust claims (getting these wrong is a credibility risk, not just a UX miss):
- **Zeffy (the donation processor WEF already uses) supports monthly/recurring giving** — confirmed by the client. Its own form page only showed a generic amount field on first load when checked remotely (likely a later step in a JS-driven flow), so the exact frequency-selection mechanics inside Zeffy were not independently verified — the client's confirmation is what this was built against.
- **No Zakat eligibility** and **no third-party charity ratings** — both confirmed absent. No claims about either were added anywhere.

Built into `public/index.php`'s `.donate-action-card` (homepage donate section) accordingly:
- A **Monthly / One-time frequency toggle**, defaulting to **Monthly** — per the M+R Benchmarks research in §2, a modest recurring gift outraises a larger one-time gift within months, and defaulting the toggle is a well-established, low-cost, high-leverage change.
- **Two separate amount ladders**: monthly ($10/$20/$35/$50/$100, closer to the ~$24 average monthly gift the research found) and one-time (the pre-existing $25/$50/$100/$250/$500).
- **Per-amount, per-frequency impact statements** (`initDonationTabs()` in `public/js/main.js`) — already existed for one-time amounts before this change; extended to monthly amounts using the same "tie each ask to a specific, different outcome" pattern the research found lifts conversion (NextAfter A/B test cited in §2: +39.1%).
- Button label, prompt copy and footer note all switch with frequency (e.g. "Become a Monthly Donor" vs "Support Our Mission").
- The CTA still links to the same general Zeffy form URL for both frequencies — no attempt was made to construct a URL parameter to pre-select frequency on Zeffy's side, since that parameter's existence/name was not verified and a wrong guess would silently fail. The footer note tells the donor they'll choose the frequency again on Zeffy's own form.

**Also fixed in passing while touching this component:** `.donate-action-card`'s `box-shadow` referenced an undefined `--shadow-2xl` token — an unfallback-ed `var()` in a `box-shadow` value list invalidates the whole declaration, so the card was likely rendering with no shadow at all. Added `--shadow-2xl` to the token scale (continues the existing sm/md/lg/xl progression) rather than just substituting `--shadow-xl`, since the author's evident intent (referencing something *past* `xl`) was a stronger shadow than any existing step provided.

**Still open:** the Zeffy CTA doesn't pre-fill the selected amount either (same "don't guess an unverified URL parameter" reasoning) — a donor who picks $50 still has to re-enter it on Zeffy's form. Worth fixing once Zeffy's actual URL/embed API is confirmed (their own docs, or the client's Zeffy dashboard, rather than guessing).

## 5. Technical reality

Stack: **PHP 8 + MySQL + vanilla CSS + vanilla JS.** No framework, no build step for the app itself. Leaflet for maps. A hand-rolled CMS.

```
WEF/
├── public/          ← document root. Pages, css, js, assets, api, admin
├── includes/        ← shared backend: db, content readers, layout partials
├── config/          ← config.php (gitignored, real credentials)
├── storage/         ← backups, logs, applications
├── docs/            ← this file and others
├── tools/           ← build + maintenance scripts
└── extras/          ← archived, not part of the running site
```

Everything outside `public/` is **absent from the served tree**, not merely denied — that is the point, and it exists because a `.git` exposure was found in an August 2026 audit.

**Database:** 13 InnoDB tables, utf8mb4. `programs`, `team_members`, `sponsors` + `sponsor_groups`, `gallery_items`, `annual_reports`, `project_updates`, `site_settings` (singleton), `form_submissions`, `content_blocks`, `admin_users`, `login_attempts`, `activity_logs`. Indexing is genuinely good — every soft-deleted table has matched `_public` and `_admin` composite indexes.

**Content model, two systems:**
- **DB lists** — `publishedPrograms()`, `publishedTeamBySection()`, `publishedSponsorGroups()`, `publishedGalleryItems()`, `publishedReports()`, `publishedUpdates()`, `siteSettings()` in `includes/content.php`. SELECT-only; public pages never include anything from `admin/`.
- **Content blocks** — `BLOCK_REGISTRY` in `includes/content-blocks.php` holds 22 pages / 65 blocks of default copy. The DB stores *overrides only*, so an empty table renders correctly and "reset" is a row delete. Read via `block()`, `blockRich()`, `blockBody()`.

Both fall back to `includes/static-data.php` when MySQL is unreachable, so the site renders with the database down.

**Security posture is solid** and should not be casually modified: session hardening with `X-Forwarded-Proto` awareness (deliberately for Hostinger's TLS-terminating proxy), 1h idle / 12h absolute lifetimes, DB re-validation every 300s, layered rate limiting including a password-spray detector, CSRF on all forms, `password_hash` + rehash-on-login, a capability whitelist where a typo denies rather than passes. Uploads sniff real MIME with `finfo`, discard the client filename, and write random names into a PHP-disabled directory.

Gaps: no 2FA, no password-reset flow.

## 6. Deployment target

**Hostinger shared hosting, PHP + MySQL.** This supersedes the previous Vercel static deployment, which is being abandoned.

Consequences that matter:

- The static `.html` mirrors are **obsolete**. `tools/build-static-site.php` generated 25 pages into *both* `public/*.html` and the repo root — 2.8 MB of byte-identical duplicates against 239 KB of real PHP source. On Hostinger they would shadow the `.php` routes.
- `vercel.json` (×2), `.vercelignore`, and the `api/contact.json` static stubs are dead weight; the real PHP endpoint returns.
- Point the domain document root at `public/`.
- `config/config.php` must be created from the sample with hPanel credentials; `APP_BASE_URL` and `CC_REDIRECT_URI` must be updated and the redirect URI re-registered with Constant Contact.
- Verify after deploy: `.htaccess` is honoured (LiteSpeed does), a `.php` file under `admin/uploads/` returns 403, `upload_max_filesize` ≥ 15 MB for report PDFs, and `gd` has WebP support.
- `public/admin/uploads/` is committed to git **on purpose** — it is the only backup. Any deploy method must carry it.

## 8. Known defects — verified, prioritised

**Severity 1 — live and harmful**

1. ✅ **Fixed 2026-09-22.** ~~`prefers-reduced-motion` does not exist in any public stylesheet.~~ `main.js:1035` contains a comment *asserting* it does — it now actually does, added globally at the top of `styles.css`.
2. ✅ **Fixed 2026-09-22.** ~~The mobile menu cannot be opened by keyboard.~~ `site-header.php`'s hamburger was a `<div role="button" tabindex="0">` with only a `click` listener; a div does not synthesise click from Enter/Space. ## 10. Working agreements

- **Verify before repeating.** This codebase has accumulated confidently-stated, wrong documentation. Grep before asserting.
- **Tokens before components.** Do not add a magic number. If the scale lacks a value, fix the scale.
- **No new `!important`.** Removing them is the goal; adding one means the cascade is wrong.
- **Mobile is the conversion path.** 43% of gifts, 30–40% worse conversion. Test on real hardware — sticky hover, tap delay, `100vh`, safe areas and the software keyboard do not reproduce in desktop device emulation.
- **Every motion respects `prefers-reduced-motion`.**
- **Ethical storytelling rules in §3 are constraints, not preferences.**
- **Never write a literal `?>` inside a PHP comment** (`//` or `#`), including one describing a bug that involves PHP tags - a `//` comment ends at the first `?>` it contains regardless of comment context, silently exiting PHP mode. `php -l` does not catch this; it is syntactically valid PHP that just stops executing where you didn't expect. Describe tag syntax in prose instead of writing it literally. Cost real time twice in one session: once as the original bug, once again writing the comment that explained it.
- **Ad-hoc headless-browser CLI screenshots are unreliable for viewport-specific verification.** Confirmed 2026-09-22: `msedge --headless --window-size=390,700 --screenshot=...` did not reliably set the actual layout viewport (`window.innerWidth` measured 504px against a requested 390px) and the output PNG silently cropped to the requested canvas size rather than the actual (wider) layout - content correctly positioned at, say, x:425 in the real 504px-wide layout was cut out of a 390px-wide image with no error or warning. This produced a false "critical bug" finding that cost significant time to run down. If a screenshot shows something missing that direct `getComputedStyle`/`getBoundingClientRect` inspection says is present, correctly positioned and correctly colored, trust the direct inspection and suspect the tool - and prefer direct inspection (or a properly configured Playwright/device-emulation setup) over ad-hoc headless CLI flags for viewport-specific claims going forward.

**Phase 4.1 (gating all `:hover` effects behind `@media (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)`)** was the largest single mechanical effort: 129 rules across `styles.css` (117), `uiverse-cards.css` (2), `impact-goals-new.css` (4), and 6 inline `<style>` blocks across 4 PHP files. A full-AST parse/rewrite/re-stringify approach (the `css` npm package) was built, verified for zero semantic diff via round-trip, then rejected before being applied - `git diff --stat` showed 1263 changed lines (the stringifier drops blank lines and relocates inline comments, touching unrelated regions like the `:root` token block) against a codebase where large, hard-to-review diffs are exactly the failure mode `docs/RESPONSIVE-PLAN.md` was trying to avoid. Replaced with a hybrid: whole-rule and mixed-selector hover blocks split by hand (each split keeps any non-hover selector in place and moves only the `:hover` selector into a new gated block), then a line-splice script wrapped the remaining runs of consecutive hover-only rules using the CSS AST purely for line-range lookup, never for output - net diff 261 lines. Verified with Chrome DevTools Protocol's `CSS.forcePseudoState`, not real mouse/touch synthesis (a real `mouse.down()` sequence on an `<a>`-tag card risked triggering navigation mid-test and did once, with the element becoming unqueryable).

**Phase 4.2 (press feedback)** added `:active` states (`transform: scale(...)`, ~100ms) to `.btn`, `.about-card`, `.project-card` and `#nav-donate-btn`, now that hover no longer doubles as a touch-press indicator per 4.1.

**Phase 4.3 (the reveal system)** fixed three problems at once (see defect #18): `.reveal` no longer re-animates on scroll-up (`unobserve()` after first reveal, mirroring `initCounterAnimation()`'s existing pattern); `.reveal`/`.text-animate-char`/the impact-goals hero-summary-CTA no longer stay permanently invisible with JS disabled or blocked (an inline, synchronous `document.documentElement.classList.add('js')` in `head-meta.php`'s `<head>`, before first paint, plus a `html:not(.js) ... { opacity: 1 !important }` fallback - and every `init*()` call in `main.js`'s `DOMContentLoaded` handler wrapped in its own try/catch, so one function throwing can no longer block the two that actually reveal content); and `.reveal-left`/`.reveal-right`'s pre-animation excursion reduced from 60px to 24px, fixing `.pb-sidebar`'s 20px overflow at 1024/1280px (defect item Q), which had nowhere to absorb a full 60px slide-in. **The one real regression of this session**, self-caught rather than reported: the first implementation scoped the hidden `opacity:0` state itself under `html.js` instead of adding a separate `:not(.js)` fallback, which raised `.reveal`/the impact-goals hero's specificity enough to silently start beating `.about-card:hover`/`:active` and `.summary-card:hover`'s own transform rules at the same specificity these had always tied with (before, source order broke the tie correctly; after, the new `html.js` prefix won outright) - found only because the *previously-passing* hover/press-feedback checks from 4.1/4.2 were re-run as a sanity check after the reveal fix, not because anything about the reveal fix itself failed its own test. Reverted to the current architecture (base rules byte-identical to their pre-session form; visibility forced only via a separate, `opacity`-only fallback that no `:hover`/`:active` rule anywhere on the site also touches, so it has nothing left to conflict with). **Working lesson, written into a code comment at the site of the fix:** a CSS change that raises one rule's specificity to fix a visibility bug can silently break an unrelated, previously-correct cascade tie elsewhere - after any specificity-affecting change, re-run the full set of *previously-passing* checks, not just a test of the bug just fixed.
